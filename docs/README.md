# OctoAcme Project Management Process Overview

This repository centralizes the core project management processes used by OctoAcme to guide teams from idea validation through delivery, release, and improvement. The approach is built around a clear lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective learning. During initiation, teams confirm the business need, identify stakeholders, define success metrics, and create a lightweight one-pager with problem statement, goals, risks, and resource needs. Once approved, the project moves into planning where the team creates a backlog, estimates scope, defines acceptance criteria, aligns milestones, and identifies dependencies and risks before work begins.

OctoAcme uses clearly defined roles to keep cross-functional delivery coordinated and accountable. Product managers define the customer value and outcomes, project managers coordinate planning, schedules, communication, and risk tracking, developers implement and test features, QA validates acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide strategic input and approvals. The operating principles emphasize customer-first thinking, iterative delivery, clear ownership, data-informed decisions, and psychological safety. These roles and principles help teams maintain alignment while moving work through the project lifecycle with transparency and shared accountability.

Communication is a consistent part of the process and happens through recurring rhythms and escalation paths. OctoAcme recommends weekly PM/Product alignment, twice-weekly standups for delivery teams, monthly stakeholder updates, and ad-hoc escalation for blockers or dependencies that affect milestones. Teams are expected to maintain a single source of truth for status and risk, using project documentation and risk registers to keep stakeholders informed. If a blocker becomes critical, escalation follows a clear path from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and Sponsor as needed—especially for business-impacting and security-related issues.

Quality and release practices are built into the workflow rather than treated as a final step. The process includes small PRs, issue linkage, CI validation, security scanning, approvals before merge, and testing at multiple levels, including unit tests, integration tests, smoke tests, and manual QA when appropriate. Release management adds staging validation, rollback planning, and post-deploy verification to reduce production risk. After each sprint or milestone, the team conducts retrospectives to capture lessons learned and convert them into action items with owners and due dates, reinforcing a culture of continuous improvement across the project lifecycle.
